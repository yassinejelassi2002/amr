\chapter{Market Overview and Regional Viability}

\section{Global and Regional Market Size}

Global AMR market (2025): USD 2.3--4.9 billion, 14--16\% CAGR \cite{valuates_agv_amr}. Warehousing and manufacturing lead; healthcare fastest-growing.

Adjacent segments: retail robotics ~USD 20B (2024) $\rightarrow$ ~USD 181B by 2031 \cite{gii_retail2030}; MEA industrial robotics ~USD 1.45B (2024) $\rightarrow$ ~USD 4.29B by 2030 (~19\% CAGR) \cite{grandview_mea}. Regional growth is real but concentrated in GCC and high-labor-cost segments.

\section{Is the Market Viable in Tunisia / North Africa / MENA?}

\subsection{Favorable Factors}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Large startup ecosystem ($>$1,000 active, top-five in MENA) \cite{lucidity_tunisia2025,startupgenome_tunisia,startuplist_tunisia}.
    \item Startup Act: tax benefits, foreign-currency access, simplified procedures.
    \item Policy encourages robotics \cite{sixwresearch_tunisia,meanceo_funding}.
    \item Local robot manufacturing proven: TIRA Robots (arms), Enova Robotics (security UGV, exported) \cite{wamda_tira}.
    \item Talented engineering pool (~10,000 new engineers/year) at competitive wages.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unfavorable Factors}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Brain drain: many engineers leave for Europe.
    \item No mature domestic robotics industry; global leaders dominate.
    \item Core components (motors, LiDAR, batteries, compute) are imported -- cost and lead-time issues.
    \item No local automation subsidy; EU-funded modernization pilots are more feasible.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strategic Implication}

Copying an international AMR and selling cheaper is not viable because high-value components are imported. The opportunity is in modular design, local software, support, and pricing (rental, sharing, usage-based). Build hybrid: import only critical components, fabricate chassis and modules locally -- this underpins local support advantages.
