\chapter{Pricing Models}

\section{How Competitors Price}

Two models: outright purchase and RaaS. Purchase prices: MiR USD 30--45k \cite{mir250}; Robotnik from EUR 50k \cite{robotnik_kairos}; Spot USD 75k+ \cite{standardbots_amr}; SMP USD 120--320k \cite{mordor_security}. Large fleets cost millions. RaaS: Locus and Cobalt charge subscriptions (Cobalt USD 3.5--6.5k/mo), shifting to opex \cite{builtin_raas,coherent_security}.

\section{Why Outright Purchase Fails Locally}

The issue is the cost-to-labor ratio, not sticker price. Against low local wages, payback is longer; upfront cash and currency exposure make purchase difficult for SMEs.

\section{Recommended Pricing for Local Market}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RaaS/leasing}: customer pays from opex \cite{builtin_raas}.
    \item \textbf{Usage-based}: per round, hour, delivery -- aligns cost with value.
    \item \textbf{Shared deployments}: multiple SMEs share one robot -- no competitor offers this.
    \item \textbf{Pilot-first}: free/low-cost pilots to generate references quickly -- first reference worth more than first margin.
\end{itemize}
